# Beginner tutorial series

Start with the complete encounter workflow, then teach each major feature with
separate web and iOS episodes where supported. Use the reference film's pale sage
background, dark teal typography, roomy layouts, and authentic app captures.

1. **Your first encounter**: record/upload → processing → review → edit/save →
   complete → discharge → follow-up. Briefly mention included general SOAP and
   discharge templates, community templates, and creating your own while choosing
   templates. Demonstrate the general templates in the overview; reserve setup
   and customization for a dedicated tutorial. Produce a web tutorial, an iOS tutorial, and
   a hybrid version once matching capture fixtures establish continuity.
2. **Record and recover**: recording, audio uploads, interruption recovery, and
   retrying an unfinished upload. Show platform-specific behavior explicitly.
3. **Review with confidence**: transcript/audio comparison, editing, full screen,
   formatting, saving, and completion. Show the transcript panel and audio playback
   with a real correction; demonstrate full screen as a focused reading/editing step.
4. **Make templates your own**: clinical note templates, discharge templates,
   community browsing/duplication, creating your own, importing existing material,
   and keeping reusable client education separate.
5. **Discharge and follow-up**: review before sending, recipient selection, email
   templates, reply routing, and the inbox.
6. **Keep the practice organized**: patients, encounter history, Today/dashboard,
   search, and team workflows. Show the past-encounters panel in patient context;
   cover Messages in the follow-up episode instead of touring every tab in the overview.
7. **Sentinel**: explain flagged encounters and review, using the documented scope
   and without implying automatic outside contact.

## Overview scope

Show a complete useful task, not every control in the web interface. The overview
introduces template options in one short passage and shows a simple edit, completion,
and follow-up. It does not explain the whole template editor, every transcript/playback
control, full-screen shortcuts, message assignment, or history navigation.

Dedicated tutorials teach those details at a readable pace using real interactions.
Keep the viewer oriented when switching panels, and show the action's result before
moving on. Dashboard, search, patient history, practice/team settings, and other web
navigation belong in focused follow-ups rather than lengthening the first video.

Do not display “AI narration” in the video footer. Keep the fictional-data label and
any applicable processing-time or static-capture disclosures accurate.

## Discharge sending and Nest

The overview must distinguish sending from reply routing: every account can send
reviewed discharge notes. Without Nest, discharge sending uses no-reply by default.
Nest adds a private relay address, hides the clinician's personal email, and brings
encounter-linked replies back into noctune. Explain this at the discharge-to-follow-up
transition, not only in an upgrade or settings tutorial. In the recorded version,
show the Discharge Notes tab before the send dialog, then demonstrate the reply-route
selection and the resulting encounter-linked message.

Use “private relay” in narration. The dedicated messaging tutorial should demonstrate
Nest, no-reply, and personal-email routes, including the address-visibility tradeoff.
Verify each route against the specific composer used: the generic messaging docs and
some discharge handlers differ on personal-email entitlement checks. Do not
promise that option in this overview or imply that every non-Nest send behaves the same.

Both video delivery modes are standard: a burned-caption LinkedIn export and a clean
video with switchable captions for docs. Keep the two exports on the same narration
and scene timeline.
