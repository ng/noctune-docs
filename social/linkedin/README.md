# LinkedIn posts for the tutorial series

One post accompanies each tutorial video in [the series plan](../../tutorials/series-plan.md).
Posts speak to veterinarians, technicians, and practice managers about the work the video
removes, then ask a question they can answer from their own clinic. The goal is conversation,
not a product announcement: with no existing audience, comments are what put a post in front of
the next person.

Each file contains the post, a first comment carrying the docs link, and extra questions for

## Before posting

- Attach the **burned-caption** export (`walkthrough-captioned.mp4`). Most feed video plays muted.
- Upload the video natively. Do not paste a YouTube or docs link in the post body; put the docs
  link in the first comment instead.
- Watch the final cut and confirm every feature the post mentions appears in it. If a scene was
  cut, remove that line from the post.
- Keep claims to what the docs state. Do not add time-saved figures, accuracy rates, or clinical
  outcomes unless we have measured them.

## Growing from zero followers

- Post from the founder's personal profile first; personal posts reach further than a new company
  page. Reshare to the company page afterward.
- Stay in the thread for the first hour and reply to every comment with a follow-up question from
  the file. A reply that asks something back doubles the conversation.
- Before posting, leave thoughtful comments on five to ten recent posts from veterinarians or vet
  communities. People click through to profiles that add something to their feed.
- Post one episode a week, Tuesday through Thursday morning in US time zones.
- Ask questions clinicians can answer from experience. Avoid "comment YES" or "tag three friends"
  prompts; LinkedIn demotes engagement bait, and vets see through it.
- Keep hashtags to three.

## Writing new posts

1. Open with a moment from clinic life, in two short lines. This is all that shows before "see
   more".
2. Name the problem in the clinician's words, then show what the video demonstrates.
3. State one limit or caution honestly (for example, that the draft needs clinical review).
4. End with one specific question. Save other questions for the first-hour replies.
5. Use lowercase `noctune` and fictional patients only.
